# SQL Views and Data Visualization Tasks
## Building Business Intelligence Through Database Views

### Learning Objectives

Through these progressive tasks, you will master the art of transforming raw retail data into meaningful business insights. You'll learn how database views serve as the foundation for business intelligence, enabling stakeholders to make data-driven decisions without needing to understand complex SQL queries.

Think of views as creating different "lenses" through which business users can examine the same underlying data. A store manager might need to see hourly sales patterns, while a regional director needs weekly summaries across multiple locations. Views let you create these different perspectives once and reuse them consistently.

The progression of these tasks mirrors real-world analytics development: starting with simple data exploration, building foundational metrics, creating time-series analysis, and culminating in sophisticated business intelligence views that power executive dashboards.

## Task 1: Data Exploration and Basic Views
**Estimated Time**: 2-3 hours  
**Difficulty**: Beginner to Intermediate  
**Learning Goal**: Understand your data structure and create foundational views for exploration

### Understanding the Foundation

Before building complex analytics, you need to thoroughly understand your data. This isn't just about knowing column names—you need to understand the business meaning behind each field, the relationships between different data points, and the patterns that drive retail operations.

Think of this phase as becoming a detective investigating your own dataset. Every query you write should answer a specific business question and reveal insights about customer behavior, operational patterns, or market dynamics.

#### Task 1.1: Create Data Exploration Views

Your first challenge is creating views that help you understand the scope and quality of your data. These views will serve as diagnostic tools throughout your project development.

**Create a Data Summary View**

```sql
-- This view provides a high-level overview of your dataset
-- Think of it as the "executive summary" of your data
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW data_overview AS
SELECT 
    -- Time span analysis helps understand the temporal scope
    MIN(dt) as earliest_date,
    MAX(dt) as latest_date,
    (MAX(dt) - MIN(dt)) as time_span_days,
    
    -- Volume metrics show the scale of your dataset
    COUNT(*) as total_records,
    COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) as unique_stores,
    COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as unique_products,
    COUNT(DISTINCT city_id) as unique_cities,
    
    -- Business metrics provide insight into the commercial scope
    ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales_value,
    ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as average_sale_amount,
    
    -- Data quality indicators help identify potential issues
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN sale_amount = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as zero_sales_percentage,
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_percentage,
    
    -- Completeness metrics show data quality
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN avg_temperature IS NOT NULL THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as weather_data_completeness
FROM raw_sales_data;
```

**Understanding Challenge**: Query this view and interpret each metric. What does a high zero_sales_percentage suggest about your business? How might weather_data_completeness affect your model's ability to predict sales patterns?

**Create a Store Characteristics View**

```sql
-- This view helps you understand the diversity and patterns across different stores
-- Retail businesses often have stores with very different characteristics
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW store_characteristics AS
SELECT 
    store_id,
    city_id,
    
    -- Temporal patterns reveal operational insights
    COUNT(DISTINCT DATE(dt)) as days_active,
    MIN(dt) as first_sale_date,
    MAX(dt) as last_sale_date,
    
    -- Volume metrics indicate store size and activity
    COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as product_variety,
    
    -- Financial performance metrics
    ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
    ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_transaction_value,
    ROUND((SUM(sale_amount) / COUNT(DISTINCT DATE(dt)))::numeric, 2) as avg_daily_sales,
    
    -- Operational efficiency indicators
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_rate,
    ROUND(AVG(discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_rate,
    
    -- Activity patterns help understand customer behavior
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN is_weekend = 1 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as weekend_activity_percentage
FROM raw_sales_data
GROUP BY store_id, city_id
ORDER BY total_sales DESC;
```

**Business Analysis Exercise**: Using this view, identify your top 5 and bottom 5 performing stores. What patterns do you notice? Do high-performing stores have different characteristics like product variety or operational efficiency?

#### Task 1.2: Product Analysis Views

Understanding product performance is crucial for retail analytics. Different products behave differently across seasons, promotions, and customer segments.

**Create a Product Performance View**

```sql
-- This view analyzes product performance across multiple dimensions
-- Product analytics drive inventory decisions and marketing strategies
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW product_performance AS
SELECT 
    product_id,
    first_category_id,
    second_category_id,
    third_category_id,
    
    -- Market presence metrics
    COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) as stores_carrying_product,
    COUNT(DISTINCT city_id) as cities_with_sales,
    COUNT(DISTINCT DATE(dt)) as days_sold,
    
    -- Sales performance indicators
    COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
    ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_revenue,
    ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_sale_amount,
    
    -- Demand patterns help with inventory planning
    ROUND((SUM(sale_amount) / COUNT(DISTINCT store_id))::numeric, 2) as revenue_per_store,
    ROUND((COUNT(*) / COUNT(DISTINCT store_id))::numeric, 2) as transactions_per_store,
    
    
    -- Promotional sensitivity
    ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN discount > 0 THEN sale_amount ELSE NULL END)::numeric, 2) as avg_discounted_sale,
    ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN discount = 0 THEN sale_amount ELSE NULL END)::numeric, 2) as avg_regular_sale,
    
    -- Stock availability impact
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt > 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as in_stock_percentage
FROM raw_sales_data
GROUP BY product_id, first_category_id, second_category_id, third_category_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10  -- Filter out products with very few sales
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
```

**Analytical Thinking Exercise**: Compare avg_discounted_sale with avg_regular_sale for different products. What does this tell you about price sensitivity? How might this inform promotional strategies?

#### Task 1.3: Time-Based Analysis Views

```sql
-- This view reveals when customers prefer to shop

CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW hourly_sales_patterns AS
```

#### Task 2.1: Sales Performance Dashboard Views

Business stakeholders need real-time visibility into sales performance across multiple dimensions. These views provide the foundation for executive dashboards and operational monitoring.

**Create a Daily Sales Performance View**

```sql
-- This view provides daily sales insights for operational management
-- It helps answer: "How are we performing today compared to our targets and historical trends?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW daily_sales_performance AS
SELECT 
    DATE(dt) as sales_date,
    
    -- Basic performance metrics
    COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) as stores_with_sales,
    COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as products_sold,
    ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
    ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_transaction_value,
    
    -- Comparative metrics help identify trends
    ROUND((SUM(sale_amount) / COUNT(DISTINCT store_id))::numeric, 2) as sales_per_store,
    ROUND((COUNT(*) / COUNT(DISTINCT store_id))::numeric, 2) as transactions_per_store,
    
    -- Day-of-week patterns for seasonality analysis
    EXTRACT(DOW FROM dt) as day_of_week,
    CASE 
        WHEN EXTRACT(DOW FROM dt) IN (0, 6) THEN 'Weekend'
        ELSE 'Weekday'
    END as day_type,
    
    -- Operational efficiency indicators
    ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_percentage,
    ROUND(AVG(discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_offered,
    
    -- Promotional impact analysis
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN discount > 0 THEN sale_amount ELSE 0 END)::numeric, 2) as promotional_sales,
    ROUND((SUM(CASE WHEN discount > 0 THEN sale_amount ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / SUM(sale_amount))::numeric, 2) as promotional_sales_percentage,
    
    -- Weather impact (when available)
    ROUND(AVG(avg_temperature)::numeric, 2) as avg_temperature,
    ROUND(AVG(precpt)::numeric, 2) as avg_precipitation
FROM raw_sales_data
GROUP BY DATE(dt), EXTRACT(DOW FROM dt)
ORDER BY sales_date DESC;
```

**Business Intelligence Exercise**: Use this view to identify your best and worst performing days. Can you correlate performance with day of week, weather conditions, or promotional activity? What actionable insights can you derive?

**Create a Store Comparison View**

```sql
-- This view enables fair comparison between stores by normalizing for different factors
-- It helps answer: "Which stores are truly outperforming when we account for their different circumstances?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW store_performance_comparison AS
WITH store_basics AS (
    SELECT 
        store_id,
        city_id,
        COUNT(DISTINCT DATE(dt)) as operating_days,
        MIN(DATE(dt)) as first_sales_date,
        MAX(DATE(dt)) as last_sales_date
    FROM raw_sales_data
    GROUP BY store_id, city_id
),
store_metrics AS (
    SELECT 
        s.store_id,
        s.city_id,
        s.operating_days,
        
        -- Volume metrics
        COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
        COUNT(DISTINCT r.product_id) as product_variety,
        
        -- Revenue metrics
        ROUND(SUM(r.sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
        ROUND((SUM(r.sale_amount) / s.operating_days)::numeric, 2) as avg_daily_sales,
        ROUND(AVG(r.sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_transaction_value,
        
        -- Operational efficiency
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN r.stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_rate,
        ROUND(AVG(r.discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_rate,
        
        -- Customer behavior patterns
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN r.is_weekend = 1 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as weekend_transaction_rate
    FROM store_basics s
    JOIN raw_sales_data r ON s.store_id = r.store_id
    GROUP BY s.store_id, s.city_id, s.operating_days
)
SELECT 
    store_id,
    city_id,
    operating_days,
    total_transactions,
    product_variety,
    total_sales,
    avg_daily_sales,
    avg_transaction_value,
    stockout_rate,
    avg_discount_rate,
    weekend_transaction_rate,
    
    -- Ranking metrics for comparison
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY total_sales DESC) as sales_rank,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY avg_daily_sales DESC) as daily_sales_rank,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY avg_transaction_value DESC) as avg_value_rank,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY stockout_rate ASC) as efficiency_rank,
    
    -- Performance categories
    CASE 
        WHEN total_sales >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.8) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY total_sales) FROM store_metrics) THEN 'High Performer'
        WHEN total_sales >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.4) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY total_sales) FROM store_metrics) THEN 'Average Performer'
        ELSE 'Needs Improvement'
    END as performance_category
FROM store_metrics
ORDER BY total_sales DESC;
```

**Strategic Analysis Challenge**: Identify stores that rank high in sales but low in efficiency (high stockout rates). What interventions might improve their performance? Conversely, find efficient stores with lower sales—how might they increase volume while maintaining efficiency?

#### Task 2.2: Product Category Analysis Views

Product category analysis reveals which product lines drive business success and helps inform inventory and marketing strategies.

**Create a Category Performance View**

```sql
-- This view analyzes performance across the product hierarchy
-- It helps answer: "Which product categories are most profitable and why?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW category_performance_analysis AS
WITH category_metrics AS (
    SELECT 
        first_category_id,
        second_category_id,
        
        -- Market presence and reach
        COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) as stores_carrying_category,
        COUNT(DISTINCT city_id) as cities_with_sales,
        COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as products_in_category,
        
        -- Sales volume and revenue
        COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
        ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_revenue,
        ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_transaction_value,
        
        -- Profitability analysis (considering discounts as margin impact)
        ROUND(SUM(sale_amount * (1 - COALESCE(discount, 0)))::numeric, 2) as revenue_after_discounts,
        ROUND(AVG(discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_rate,
        
        -- Operational metrics
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_rate,
        ROUND(AVG(stock_hour6_22_cnt)::numeric, 2) as avg_stock_level,
        
        -- Seasonal and promotional sensitivity
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN is_weekend = 1 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as weekend_sales_percentage,
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN holiday_flag = true THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as holiday_sales_percentage,
        
        -- Weather sensitivity analysis
        ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN avg_temperature > 25 THEN sale_amount ELSE NULL END)::numeric, 2) as hot_weather_avg_sale,
        ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN avg_temperature <= 15 THEN sale_amount ELSE NULL END)::numeric, 2) as cold_weather_avg_sale
    FROM raw_sales_data
    WHERE first_category_id IS NOT NULL AND second_category_id IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY first_category_id, second_category_id
    HAVING COUNT(*) >= 50  -- Filter for statistically meaningful categories
)
SELECT 
    first_category_id,
    second_category_id,
    stores_carrying_category,
    cities_with_sales,
    products_in_category,
    total_transactions,
    total_revenue,
    avg_transaction_value,
    revenue_after_discounts,
    avg_discount_rate,
    stockout_rate,
    avg_stock_level,
    weekend_sales_percentage,
    holiday_sales_percentage,
    hot_weather_avg_sale,
    cold_weather_avg_sale,
    
    -- Performance rankings within category hierarchy
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY first_category_id ORDER BY total_revenue DESC) as rank_within_primary_category,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY total_revenue DESC) as overall_revenue_rank,
    
    -- Revenue concentration analysis
    ROUND((total_revenue * 100.0 / SUM(total_revenue) OVER ())::numeric, 2) as revenue_share_percentage,
    
    -- Performance classification
    CASE 
        WHEN total_revenue >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.75) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY total_revenue) FROM category_metrics) 
             AND stockout_rate <= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.25) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY stockout_rate) FROM category_metrics)
        THEN 'High Performance & Efficient'
        WHEN total_revenue >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.75) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY total_revenue) FROM category_metrics)
        THEN 'High Revenue but Operational Issues'
        WHEN stockout_rate <= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.25) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY stockout_rate) FROM category_metrics)
        THEN 'Efficient but Low Revenue'
        ELSE 'Needs Improvement'
    END as performance_classification,
    
    -- Weather sensitivity indicator
    CASE 
        WHEN hot_weather_avg_sale IS NOT NULL AND cold_weather_avg_sale IS NOT NULL THEN
            ROUND(((hot_weather_avg_sale - cold_weather_avg_sale) / cold_weather_avg_sale * 100)::numeric, 2)
        ELSE NULL
    END as weather_sensitivity_percentage
FROM category_metrics
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
```

**Market Intelligence Exercise**: Identify categories that show high weather sensitivity. How might this inform seasonal inventory planning? Which categories have the highest revenue share but also high stockout rates—what does this suggest about demand forecasting accuracy?

#### Task 2.3: Time Series Analysis Views

Time series analysis is crucial for understanding trends, seasonality, and forecasting future performance.

**Create a Weekly Sales Trend View**

```sql
-- This view aggregates sales data by week to identify trends and seasonality
-- It helps answer: "Are we growing, declining, or stable over time?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW weekly_sales_trends AS
WITH weekly_aggregates AS (
    SELECT 
        DATE_TRUNC('week', dt)::date as week_start_date,
        EXTRACT(WEEK FROM dt) as week_number,
        EXTRACT(YEAR FROM dt) as year,
        
        -- Core metrics
        COUNT(*) as total_transactions,
        COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) as active_stores,
        COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as products_sold,
        ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
        ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_transaction_value,
        
        -- Operational metrics
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_rate,
        ROUND(AVG(discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_rate,
        
        -- Promotional and seasonal indicators
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN holiday_flag = true THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as holiday_transaction_rate,
        ROUND(AVG(avg_temperature)::numeric, 2) as avg_temperature,
        ROUND(AVG(precpt)::numeric, 2) as avg_precipitation
    FROM raw_sales_data
    GROUP BY DATE_TRUNC('week', dt), EXTRACT(WEEK FROM dt), EXTRACT(YEAR FROM dt)
)
SELECT 
    week_start_date,
    week_number,
    year,
    total_transactions,
    active_stores,
    products_sold,
    total_sales,
    avg_transaction_value,
    stockout_rate,
    avg_discount_rate,
    holiday_transaction_rate,
    avg_temperature,
    avg_precipitation,
    
    -- Trend analysis using window functions
    LAG(total_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date) as previous_week_sales,
    ROUND(((total_sales - LAG(total_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date)) * 100.0 / 
           LAG(total_sales, 1) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date))::numeric, 2) as week_over_week_growth,
    
    -- Moving averages for trend smoothing
    ROUND(AVG(total_sales) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date ROWS BETWEEN 3 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)::numeric, 2) as four_week_moving_avg,
    ROUND(AVG(total_sales) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date ROWS BETWEEN 11 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)::numeric, 2) as twelve_week_moving_avg,
    
    -- Year-over-year comparison (when applicable)
    LAG(total_sales, 52) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date) as same_week_last_year,
    ROUND(((total_sales - LAG(total_sales, 52) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date)) * 100.0 / 
           LAG(total_sales, 52) OVER (ORDER BY week_start_date))::numeric, 2) as year_over_year_growth,
    
    -- Performance classification
    CASE 
        WHEN total_sales > AVG(total_sales) OVER () * 1.2 THEN 'Above Average'
        WHEN total_sales < AVG(total_sales) OVER () * 0.8 THEN 'Below Average'
        ELSE 'Average'
    END as performance_classification
FROM weekly_aggregates
ORDER BY week_start_date;
```

**Trend Analysis Challenge**: Use this view to identify seasonal patterns in your data. Are there specific weeks that consistently outperform or underperform? How do weather patterns correlate with sales trends?

#### Task 3.1: Customer Behavior Analysis Views

Understanding customer behavior patterns helps optimize store operations and marketing strategies.

**Create a Customer Shopping Pattern View**

```sql
-- This view analyzes shopping patterns to understand customer behavior
-- It helps answer: "When and how do customers prefer to shop, and what drives their behavior?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW customer_shopping_patterns AS
WITH hourly_patterns AS (
    SELECT 
        hour,
        day_of_week,
        CASE 
            WHEN day_of_week IN (0, 6) THEN 'Weekend'
            ELSE 'Weekday'
        END as day_type,
        
        -- Shopping intensity metrics
        COUNT(*) as transaction_count,
        ROUND(AVG(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as avg_purchase_value,
        ROUND(SUM(sale_amount)::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
        
        -- Basket composition analysis
        COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as unique_products_sold,
        ROUND((COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) * 1.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 3) as products_per_transaction,
        
        -- Price sensitivity indicators
        ROUND(AVG(discount)::numeric, 3) as avg_discount_rate,
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN discount > 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as promotional_transaction_rate,
        
        -- Stock availability impact on behavior
        ROUND((COUNT(CASE WHEN stock_hour6_22_cnt = 0 THEN 1 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*))::numeric, 2) as stockout_encounter_rate,
        
        -- Weather influence on shopping
        ROUND(AVG(avg_temperature)::numeric, 2) as avg_temperature,
        ROUND(AVG(precpt)::numeric, 2) as avg_precipitation
    FROM raw_sales_data
    GROUP BY hour, day_of_week
),
pattern_classification AS (
    SELECT *,
        -- Peak hour identification
        CASE 
            WHEN transaction_count >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.8) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY transaction_count) FROM hourly_patterns) 
            THEN 'Peak Hours'
            WHEN transaction_count <= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.2) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY transaction_count) FROM hourly_patterns)
            THEN 'Low Traffic Hours'
            ELSE 'Normal Hours'
        END as traffic_classification,
        
        -- Value classification
        CASE 
            WHEN avg_purchase_value >= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.7) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY avg_purchase_value) FROM hourly_patterns)
            THEN 'High Value Period'
            WHEN avg_purchase_value <= (SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.3) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY avg_purchase_value) FROM hourly_patterns)
            THEN 'Low Value Period'
            ELSE 'Average Value Period'
        END as value_classification
    FROM hourly_patterns
)
SELECT 
    hour,
    day_of_week,
    day_type,
    transaction_count,
    avg_purchase_value,
    total_sales,
    unique_products_sold,
    products_per_transaction,
    avg_discount_rate,
    promotional_transaction_rate,
    stockout_encounter_rate,
    avg_temperature,
    avg_precipitation,
    traffic_classification,
    value_classification,
    
    -- Operational insights
    ROUND((total_sales / SUM(total_sales) OVER (PARTITION BY day_type) * 100)::numeric, 2) as share_of_daytype_sales,
    
    -- Customer behavior patterns
    CASE 
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 6 AND 9 THEN 'Morning Rush'
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 10 AND 11 THEN 'Late Morning'
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 12 AND 14 THEN 'Lunch Period'
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 15 AND 17 THEN 'Afternoon'
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 18 AND 20 THEN 'Evening Rush'
        WHEN hour BETWEEN 21 AND 23 THEN 'Evening'
        ELSE 'Night/Early Morning'
    END as shopping_period
FROM pattern_classification
ORDER BY day_of_week, hour;
```

**Behavioral Analysis Exercise**: Use this view to identify the most profitable shopping periods. Do customers buy more expensive items at certain times? How does weather affect shopping behavior during different periods?

#### Task 3.2: Predictive Analytics Support Views

These views create features and aggregations that support machine learning model development.

**Create a Model Features View**

```sql
-- This view creates features that machine learning models can use for prediction
-- It helps answer: "What historical patterns can help predict future sales?"
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW ml_model_features AS
WITH base_features AS (
    SELECT 
        store_id,
        product_id,
        dt,
        sale_amount,
        
        -- Time-based features
        hour,
        day_of_week,
        month,
        is_weekend,
        holiday_flag,
        activity_flag,
        
        -- Product and inventory features
        stock_hour6_22_cnt,
        discount,
        
        -- Weather features
        avg_temperature,
        avg_humidity,
        avg_wind_level,
        precpt,
        
        -- Derived time features
        CASE 
            WHEN hour BETWEEN 6 AND 11 THEN 1 ELSE 0 
        END as is_morning_rush,
```

```sql
-- This view analyzes customer retention and behavior patterns over time
-- Useful for understanding customer lifecycle and loyalty trends
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW cohort_analysis AS
WITH store_first_purchase AS (
    -- Define cohorts based on first purchase date per store
    SELECT 
        store_id,
        MIN(DATE(dt)) as first_purchase_date,
        DATE_TRUNC('month', MIN(dt)) as cohort_month
    FROM raw_sales_data
    GROUP BY store_id
),
monthly_activity AS (
    -- Track monthly activity for each store
    SELECT 
        store_id,
        DATE_TRUNC('month', dt) as activity_month,
        SUM(sale_amount) as monthly_sales,
        COUNT(*) as monthly_transactions
    FROM raw_sales_data
    GROUP BY store_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', dt)
),
cohort_table AS (
    SELECT 
        sfp.cohort_month,
        ma.activity_month,
        (EXTRACT(YEAR FROM ma.activity_month) - EXTRACT(YEAR FROM sfp.cohort_month)) * 12 + 
        (EXTRACT(MONTH FROM ma.activity_month) - EXTRACT(MONTH FROM sfp.cohort_month)) as period_number,
        COUNT(DISTINCT ma.store_id) as active_stores,
        SUM(ma.monthly_sales) as cohort_sales,
        AVG(ma.monthly_sales) as avg_store_sales,
        COUNT(DISTINCT sfp.store_id) as cohort_size
    FROM store_first_purchase sfp
    LEFT JOIN monthly_activity ma ON sfp.store_id = ma.store_id
    WHERE ma.activity_month IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY sfp.cohort_month, ma.activity_month, period_number
)
SELECT 
    cohort_month,
    period_number,
    cohort_size,
    active_stores,
    ROUND((active_stores * 100.0 / cohort_size)::numeric, 2) as retention_rate,
    ROUND(cohort_sales::numeric, 2) as total_sales,
    ROUND(avg_store_sales::numeric, 2) as avg_sales_per_store,
    
    -- Cohort labels for easier interpretation
    CASE 
        WHEN period_number = 0 THEN 'Month 0 (Launch)'
        WHEN period_number = 1 THEN 'Month 1'
        WHEN period_number = 2 THEN 'Month 2'
        WHEN period_number = 3 THEN 'Month 3'
        WHEN period_number <= 6 THEN 'Months 4-6'
        WHEN period_number <= 12 THEN 'Months 7-12'
        ELSE 'Year 2+'
    END as period_label,
    
    -- Performance indicators
    CASE 
        WHEN retention_rate >= 80 THEN 'High Retention'
        WHEN retention_rate >= 60 THEN 'Medium Retention'
        ELSE 'Low Retention'
    END as retention_category
FROM cohort_table
WHERE period_number >= 0
ORDER BY cohort_month, period_number;
```

#### Task 5.2: Market Basket Analysis View

```sql
-- This view identifies products frequently bought together
-- Useful for cross-selling and product placement strategies
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW market_basket_analysis AS
WITH transaction_baskets AS (
    -- Group products purchased together (same store, same hour)
    SELECT 
        store_id,
        DATE(dt) as transaction_date,
        hour,
        ARRAY_AGG(DISTINCT product_id ORDER BY product_id) as product_basket,
        COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) as basket_size,
        SUM(sale_amount) as basket_value
    FROM raw_sales_data
    WHERE sale_amount > 0
    GROUP BY store_id, DATE(dt), hour
    HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) >= 2  -- Only multi-product baskets
),
product_pairs AS (
    -- Identify all product pairs within baskets
    SELECT 
        p1.product_id as product_a,
        p2.product_id as product_b,
        COUNT(*) as co_occurrence_count,
        AVG(tb.basket_value) as avg_basket_value,
        COUNT(DISTINCT tb.store_id) as stores_with_pair
    FROM transaction_baskets tb
    CROSS JOIN LATERAL unnest(tb.product_basket) p1(product_id)
    CROSS JOIN LATERAL unnest(tb.product_basket) p2(product_id)
    WHERE p1.product_id < p2.product_id  -- Avoid duplicates and self-pairs
    GROUP BY p1.product_id, p2.product_id
    HAVING COUNT(*) >= 5  -- Filter for statistically significant pairs
),
product_frequencies AS (
    -- Calculate individual product frequencies for lift calculation
    SELECT 
        product_id,
        COUNT(*) as individual_frequency
    FROM transaction_baskets tb
    CROSS JOIN LATERAL unnest(tb.product_basket) p(product_id)
    GROUP BY product_id
),
total_baskets AS (
    SELECT COUNT(*) as total_basket_count FROM transaction_baskets
)
SELECT 
    pp.product_a,
    pp.product_b,
    pp.co_occurrence_count,
    ROUND(pp.avg_basket_value::numeric, 2) as avg_basket_value,
    pp.stores_with_pair,
    
    -- Association rule metrics
    ROUND((pp.co_occurrence_count * 100.0 / tb.total_basket_count)::numeric, 4) as support_percentage,
    ROUND((pp.co_occurrence_count * 100.0 / pf1.individual_frequency)::numeric, 2) as confidence_a_to_b,
    ROUND((pp.co_occurrence_count * 100.0 / pf2.individual_frequency)::numeric, 2) as confidence_b_to_a,
    
    -- Lift calculation (how much more likely together than by chance)
    ROUND((pp.co_occurrence_count * tb.total_basket_count * 1.0 / 
           (pf1.individual_frequency * pf2.individual_frequency))::numeric, 2) as lift,
    
    -- Business interpretation
    CASE 
        WHEN (pp.co_occurrence_count * tb.total_basket_count * 1.0 / 
              (pf1.individual_frequency * pf2.individual_frequency)) > 2 THEN 'Strong Association'
        WHEN (pp.co_occurrence_count * tb.total_basket_count * 1.0 / 
              (pf1.individual_frequency * pf2.individual_frequency)) > 1.5 THEN 'Moderate Association'
        WHEN (pp.co_occurrence_count * tb.total_basket_count * 1.0 / 
              (pf1.individual_frequency * pf2.individual_frequency)) > 1.1 THEN 'Weak Association'
        ELSE 'No Significant Association'
    END as association_strength,
    
    -- Actionable insights
    CASE 
        WHEN (pp.co_occurrence_count * 100.0 / pf1.individual_frequency) > 30 THEN 'Consider bundling products'
        WHEN (pp.co_occurrence_count * 100.0 / pf1.individual_frequency) > 20 THEN 'Place products near each other'
        ELSE 'Monitor for promotional opportunities'
    END as recommended_action
FROM product_pairs pp
JOIN product_frequencies pf1 ON pp.product_a = pf1.product_id
JOIN product_frequencies pf2 ON pp.product_b = pf2.product_id
CROSS JOIN total_baskets tb
WHERE (pp.co_occurrence_count * tb.total_basket_count * 1.0 / 
       (pf1.individual_frequency * pf2.individual_frequency)) > 1.1  -- Only meaningful associations
ORDER BY lift DESC, co_occurrence_count DESC;
```
